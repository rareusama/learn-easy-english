How to Introduce Yourself in English

A simple self-introduction format for school, work and everyday conversations.

A self-introduction does not need difficult words. Speak slowly, use short sentences and share only details that are suitable for the situation.

اردو میں: اس سبق کو آہستہ آہستہ پڑھیں، مثالوں کو بلند آواز سے دہرائیں اور روزانہ مشق کریں۔

Simple introduction template

Hello! My name is ____. I am from ____. I am a student / I work as a ____. I enjoy learning new things. It is nice to meet you.

Urdu explanation

سلام! میرا نام ____ ہے۔ میرا تعلق ____ سے ہے۔ میں طالب علم ہوں / میں ____ کے طور پر کام کرتا/کرتی ہوں۔ مجھے نئی چیزیں سیکھنا پسند ہے۔ آپ سے مل کر خوشی ہوئی۔

Practice tips

Practise your introduction aloud. Replace the blanks with your own information. Keep it short and natural. Do not share private information with strangers.

Example: a short introduction

English: Hello, my name is Sara. I am from Lahore. I am a student. I enjoy reading and learning English. It is nice to meet you.

اردو: سلام، میرا نام سارہ ہے۔ میں لاہور سے ہوں۔ میں طالبہ ہوں۔ مجھے کتابیں پڑھنا اور انگریزی سیکھنا پسند ہے۔ آپ سے مل کر خوشی ہوئی۔

Useful phrases for different situations

I am currently studying...
میں اس وقت ... کی تعلیم حاصل کر رہا/رہی ہوں۔
I work as a...
میں ... کے طور پر کام کرتا/کرتی ہوں۔
In my free time, I like...
فارغ وقت میں مجھے ... پسند ہے۔
My goal is to...
میرا مقصد ... ہے۔
I am interested in...
مجھے ... میں دلچسپی ہے۔

Keep your introduction suitable

For a classroom, you can mention your class or subject. For a job interview, focus on relevant skills and experience. In a casual conversation, one or two details are enough. You do not need to share your home address, phone number or other private information.

Practice activity

  1. Write your name and where you are from.
  2. Add one sentence about study or work.
  3. Add one hobby or interest.
  4. Read your introduction aloud and check that each sentence is complete.
Practice task: Choose three examples from this lesson, write them in your notebook, and say them aloud. Try making one new sentence of your own.

Educational content only. Examples are for learning and practice.

Build a natural 30-second introduction

Keep your introduction relevant to the situation. In a class, mention your name, where you are from and what you are studying. In a job interview, focus more on your skills and experience.

Example: “Hello, my name is Ahmed. I am from Lahore. I am learning English because I want to communicate more confidently. In my free time, I enjoy reading and playing cricket.”

Do not memorise personal details that are not true. Practise the structure, then replace the example details with your own information.
